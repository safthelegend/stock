Standard operating procedure

Four steps, the same every run, all on the record.

The instruments

A school cannot release food without a record.

Nobody should have to take our word for it that the food arrived safe. Every box carries a temperature record from pack-out to hand-off, so a pantry can serve what we bring with confidence, and a school can show exactly what left its building.11

The fleet is designed around roughly three shared logger units rotating across routes, not one per box. A run without a unit was to be checked against a thermal model built from logged runs on that route; no such model exists, because there are no logged runs. Until there are, such a run has no temperature record and the manifest says so. There is no location sensor.

The delivery log

Every delivery, on the public record.

No annual report, no estimates. A public record of every box that moves — origin, destination, weight, temperature — sortable and filterable by anyone who wants to check our work. It opens with the first delivery and never closes.

Why we exist

Built by the students who watched the share table get thrown out.

We started this because the food on our share tables was sealed, safe, and a short walk from people who needed it. Students built the boxes, the logger and the tracker, and will walk the routes and keep the logs.

If your building has a share table and a receiving site within walking distance, it can run there too.

The limits

What this does not do. Plainly.

the mechanism

Surplus cafeteria food is a waste problem sitting next to a hunger problem. The system moves sealed, unserved items from a share table to a receiving site in the same borough, on foot, the same afternoon. That is what a borough cluster buys you; it is not a claim about crossing the city.

what it measurably changes

Pounds diverted from a school's waste stream, food in front of people who came to a pantry that week, and a public record a school can point at when asked what happened to the surplus.

what it does not touch

Income. SNAP enrollment. Rent, wages, or any of the reasons need exists in the first place. A pound recovered is not a benefit restored, and treating the two as interchangeable is how a real cut gets talked about as if it had been absorbed.

what would have to be true

Many more buildings than the one we are setting up in. A signed receiving site in every borough we claim a cluster in; three of the four have none. Routes that survive graduation and staff turnover. A health department that finds the records sufficient. Sites with capacity for same-day perishable food.

what would show it is not working

Pounds climbing while partner sites report no change in what they can hand out. Boxes arriving above the threshold. Gaps in the log, or corrections that outnumber clean entries. A cluster that never finds a receiving site and stays on the map anyway. We publish the log so this is checkable by someone other than us.
