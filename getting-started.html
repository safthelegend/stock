getting started

Starting this at your school is a route, not a signup.

This is a real operational commitment: a walkable route to a receiving partner, a signed one-page agreement, a small student team that shows up on schedule, and a delivery log someone actually checks. Nothing here happens automatically once you submit a form. Below is exactly what it takes, in order.

The sequence

Six things, roughly in this order.

The health code memo and the partner agreement gate everything after them — do those two first. The rest can move in parallel.

Pickup cadence

Start at whatever cadence you can actually keep.

Daily pickups are the mature end-state, not the entry requirement. A school running its first month should start at whatever cadence it can actually sustain — once or twice a week is a legitimate starting point, and treating it as a lesser version of the real thing gets the order of operations backward.

Consistency is what a receiving partner is actually evaluating in those first few weeks. A route that shows up reliably every Tuesday builds the trust that lets a pantry plan around you, and that trust is what eventually makes daily pickups possible. An ambitious daily commitment that collapses in three weeks costs more than it earns: the partner site stops planning around you, and starting over is harder than starting slow ever was.

Scale the schedule up after the first ten or so pickups have gone cleanly — not before.

What you'll need

Five things, before the first pickup.

People

Two or three students who can commit to the same time slot every week, not whoever happens to be free that day.

A route

A walk under thirty minutes, out and back, so the team can run it same-day and still make their bus.

A partner site

A food pantry or food bank within that walk, with capacity to take same-day perishable food.

The logger

Borrowed time on an existing fleet if you're joining a running route, or the firmware repo if you're building your own.

The agreement

One page, signed by the school and the receiving site, before the first box leaves the building.
